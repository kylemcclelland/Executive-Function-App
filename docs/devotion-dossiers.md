## DEV-001: Sign of the Cross

**Category:** Daily Prayer  ·  **Workflow status:** proposed (unpublished)  ·  **Prose:** AI-drafted, unapproved

**Short description:** A short prayer that begins and ends other prayers

**Overview (unapproved):** The Sign of the Cross is a brief prayer made by tracing a cross on the body while naming the Holy Trinity. Catholics use it to begin and end prayer, and it is made at the start of Mass.

**Historical introduction (unapproved):** Early Christian writers describe tracing a cross on the forehead as a habitual gesture; Tertullian, writing around AD 200, mentions it. Forms that name the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit developed over the following centuries.

**Devotional purpose (unapproved):** It recalls Baptism and the cross of Christ, and places prayer and daily actions under the name of the Holy Trinity.

**Traditional usage (unapproved):** at the start and end of prayer, at the start of Mass, on entering a church, before meals, and at the beginning and end of the day.

**References for the prose:**
- Tertullian, De corona militis, 3 (citation to be verified by the reviewer)
- Catechism of the Catholic Church, no. 2157 (citation to be verified by the reviewer)

## DEV-002: Our Father

**Category:** Our Lord  ·  **Workflow status:** proposed (unpublished)  ·  **Prose:** AI-drafted, unapproved

**Short description:** The prayer Jesus taught his disciples

**Overview (unapproved):** The Our Father, also called the Lord’s Prayer, is the prayer that Jesus teaches his disciples in the Gospels of Matthew and Luke. It is prayed at Mass, in the Church’s daily prayer and in the Rosary.

**Historical introduction (unapproved):** The prayer appears in two forms in the Gospels, in Matthew 6:9–13 and Luke 11:2–4. It has been used in the Church’s worship and instruction from the earliest centuries; the Didache, an early Christian text, directs that it be prayed three times a day.

**Devotional purpose (unapproved):** It is the prayer the Lord himself gave to his disciples, and the Catechism of the Catholic Church devotes its final part to it.

**Traditional usage (unapproved):** at Mass, in Morning and Evening Prayer, in the Rosary, and in personal prayer at any time.

**References for the prose:**
- Gospel of Matthew 6:9–13
- Gospel of Luke 11:2–4
- Didache, 8 (citation to be verified by the reviewer)
- Catechism of the Catholic Church, nos. 2759–2865

## DEV-003: Hail Mary

**Category:** Our Lady  ·  **Workflow status:** proposed (unpublished)  ·  **Prose:** AI-drafted, unapproved

**Short description:** A greeting to Mary and a request for her prayers

**Overview (unapproved):** The Hail Mary greets Mary with words from the Gospel of Luke and then asks for her prayers. It is the prayer repeated in the Rosary and in the Angelus.

**Historical introduction (unapproved):** The first part joins the angel’s greeting to Mary (Luke 1:28) and Elizabeth’s greeting (Luke 1:42). The petition that follows was added in later centuries, and the prayer reached its present form in the Roman Breviary of 1568.

**Devotional purpose (unapproved):** It greets Mary as the Gospel does and asks for her prayers now and at the hour of death.

**Traditional usage (unapproved):** in the Rosary and the Angelus, and in personal prayer, especially at morning, evening and in need.

**References for the prose:**
- Gospel of Luke 1:28 and 1:42
- Roman Breviary of 1568, on the standard form of the prayer (citation to be verified by the reviewer)

**Traditional usage (unapproved):** at the end of each psalm in the Liturgy of the Hours, at the end of each decade of the Rosary, and at the close of other prayers.

## DEV-005: Apostles’ Creed

**Category:** Daily Prayer  ·  **Workflow status:** proposed (unpublished)  ·  **Prose:** AI-drafted, unapproved

**Short description:** A summary of the Christian faith

**Overview (unapproved):** The Apostles’ Creed is a short statement of the Christian faith in three parts, on the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit. It is an ancient profession of faith and it begins the Rosary. Its name reflects an old tradition connecting it with the teaching of the Apostles.

**Historical introduction (unapproved):** It developed from the baptismal profession of faith of the early Roman Church. Its present form is found by about the eighth century. The tradition that each of the twelve Apostles contributed one article is a later one.

**Devotional purpose (unapproved):** It gathers the Church’s baptismal faith in the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit into a short profession that can be prayed by heart.

**Traditional usage (unapproved):** at the start of the Rosary, in the renewal of baptismal promises, and in personal prayer as a profession of faith.

**References for the prose:**
- Catechism of the Catholic Church, nos. 194–197 (citation to be verified by the reviewer)

## DEV-006: The Angelus

**Category:** Our Lady  ·  **Workflow status:** proposed (unpublished)  ·  **Prose:** AI-drafted, unapproved

**Short description:** A traditional prayer recalling the Incarnation, prayed three times a day

**Overview (unapproved):** The Angelus is a traditional prayer that recalls the Incarnation. It is customarily prayed three times a day, at morning, noon and evening, often at the ringing of a bell. In the Easter season the Regina Caeli is traditionally prayed in its place.

**Historical introduction (unapproved):** A medieval devotion rooted in the custom of reciting Hail Marys at the ringing of a bell, extended over time from the evening to morning and midday.

**Devotional purpose (unapproved):** It recalls the Incarnation, when the Word became flesh, and sanctifies the three main points of the day.

**Traditional usage (unapproved):** at dawn, at midday and at dusk, with the Regina Caeli in its place in the Easter season.
